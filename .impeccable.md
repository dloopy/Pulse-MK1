## Design Context

### Users
Students and learners practicing an instrument at home, building rhythm and timing skills through daily use. They may have limited music theory vocabulary (don't know "subdivision" by that name), are often playing while glancing at the screen, and may encounter the interface without any onboarding. The goal is rhythm improvement — they need the tool to feel encouraging and accessible, not intimidating.

### Brand Personality
Boutique pedal — hand-crafted feel, distinctive personality, the kind of gear you'd show off to a friend. Think: a small-run hardware piece you'd see at a music gear expo. Three-word personality: **warm, precise, crafted**.

### Aesthetic Direction
Skeuomorphic hardware pedal simulator. Cream/warm white chassis with amber/gold accent throughout. OLED-style dot-matrix display. DM Mono for all values and readouts, DM Sans for labels and wordmark. Corner screws, physical knobs with drag interaction, a large tactile footswitch. Committed to the analog/physical metaphor — not decorative, this IS the device.

Anti-reference: Generic SaaS dashboard with cards and charts. Dark mode with neon accents. Flat icon apps. Anything that reads as "web app."

### Design Principles
1. **The device metaphor is sacred** — every design decision should reinforce "this is a piece of gear," never break the illusion
2. **Legibility at arm's length** — learners are playing an instrument while glancing at this; labels must read at a distance, not just at a desk
3. **Progressive complexity** — the Play mode should feel simple and immediately usable; Train mode's power reveals itself gradually
4. **Amber means alive** — the amber accent is the heartbeat; use it exclusively for "active/running" states to create a clear on/off signal
5. **Boutique personality earns its place** — the brand has character; it shouldn't whisper; the wordmark, idle states, and empty states should all have voice
